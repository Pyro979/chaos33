# Art Direction Brief

## Overall Feel
Bold, clean, playful, highly readable party-game presentation.

The visual identity should emphasize the contrast between **RULE** and **ROLE** while still feeling like one cohesive game system.

Use clean flat-color fields with only a thin neutral edge where needed. Keep all clue text inside the print safe area.

---

## TAKE ME Cards

### Format
- Landscape orientation
- Pure-white (`#FFFFFF`) card background
- Near-full-bleed RULE and ROLE fields, with safe text placement inside them
- A shallow, clean angled split between the two fields
- A substantial circular **OR** badge straddling the split on the front

### Choice Layout
Each side contains:
- Large all-caps secret word
- Smaller supporting phrase

Example:

**PENGUIN**  
*by the RULES*

**SUBMARINE**  
*in a ROLE*

RULE stays on top and ROLE stays on the bottom rather than alternating.

### Visual Contrast
**RULE**
- More structured / geometric
- Cleaner edges
- Straightforward or architectural sans-serif
- RULE color

**ROLE**
- Slightly looser / more expressive
- Expressive, highly readable display typeface; it does not need to be a serif
- ROLE color

Both halves use bold flat colors.

---

## RULE Cards

### Format
- Portrait orientation
- Same basic card chassis as ROLE cards
- Solid RULE-color header
- Header says **RULE**
- Small brain icon as secondary deck identifier

### Typography
- RULE title uses the structured RULE typeface
- Body copy uses a clean shared sans-serif
- Important restrictions can use selective bolding

### Content
Primary hierarchy:

1. RULE
2. Rule title
3. Instruction text
4. Optional example / starter

Some Rules may include examples when the mechanic benefits from an immediate demonstration.

Starter language should make optionality explicit, e.g.:

**You can start with:**  
*"Good news..."*

---

## ROLE Cards

### Format
- Portrait orientation
- Same overall layout as RULE cards
- Solid ROLE-color header
- Header says **ROLE**
- Speech bubble icon as secondary deck identifier

### Typography
- ROLE title uses the expressive ROLE serif/display typeface
- Body copy uses the same clean sans-serif as RULE cards
- Starter text can be italicized or otherwise visually separated

### Content
Primary hierarchy:

1. ROLE
2. Role title
3. Premise / instruction
4. Optional starter

Starter language:

**You can start with:**  
*"I know this sounds crazy, but..."*

---

## Card Backs

### RULE / ROLE
Use the same inset-box language as the TAKE ME cards, but as a complete single-color field.

Each back should identify the deck through:
- Color
- Text
- Icon

Text can appear upright and rotated 180° so the deck is readable from either end.

**RULE**
- RULE color
- Brain icon

**ROLE**
- ROLE color
- Speech bubble icon

### TAKE ME
- Pure-white (`#FFFFFF`) background
- No OR badge needed
- Use **TAKE ME** typography as the primary graphic
- Split/color the lettering so each side visually corresponds with the RULE and ROLE sides of the card/front
- On opposite orientations, TAKE and the opposing ME share a color; the other TAKE and ME share the other color
- Slight intentional offset/asymmetry is encouraged
- Small triangle near the top edge can indicate orientation after shuffling

---

## Table Presence
Intended setup creates a visual three-part system:

**RULE deck | TAKE ME deck | ROLE deck**

Portrait + landscape + portrait creates a loose H-shaped arrangement.

Colors, fonts, and icons should make the three components feel immediately related while keeping RULE and ROLE clearly distinct.

---

## Color Direction
Current prototype palette:

- RULE: `#00A88F`
- ROLE: `#7A3FF2`
- RUMBLE: `#E8463A`
- Neutral: `#FFFFFF`

Requirements:
- Clear color differentiation
- Good text contrast
- Avoid combinations that strongly imply another theme
- RULE and ROLE colors should carry consistently across card fronts, backs, rules examples, and other UI




# Rumble Card Art Direction Brief

Rumble cards use the same back as TAKE ME cards in both ink modes.

## Overall Feel
Rumbles should feel like a sudden interruption to the normal RULE / ROLE flow.

They should be:
- Fast to parse
- Visually urgent
- Much simpler than the old duel cards
- Self-contained enough that players do not need to check the rulebook during play

---

## Format
- Portrait orientation
- Pure-white (`#FFFFFF`) body
- No perimeter border; the strong red header carries the Rumble identity
- Solid red header across the top
- Minimal decoration
- Large, highly readable text

---

## Header

Stack the header on two lines:

**RUMBLE!**  
**FIRST TO 3 WINS**

`RUMBLE!` is larger. Do not add a colon after `WINS`.

---

## Body

The body should contain only the immediate challenge. Put `Go!` on its own line
and keep the category or name on one line when it fits.

Examples:

**Name 3 JUNK DRAWER ITEMS. Go!**

**Name 3 ANIMALS starting with S. Go!**

**Name 3 famous people or fictional characters named CHRIS. Go!**

Important words or phrases can be:
- Bolded
- Larger and more prominent
- Set in the Rumble color

Avoid separate duel names such as:
- Alpha Blitz
- Theme Blitz
- Star Blitz

The challenge itself is enough.

---

## Typography
- Use the shared readable body font
- Very large challenge text
- Heavy emphasis on the variable part of the challenge
- On alphabet Rumbles, show the preprinted letter as large, unboxed type
- Keep line count low
- `Go!` can be emphasized slightly for energy

---

## Rules Kept Off the Card
The rulebook handles the universal Rumble structure:

- Players immediately to the active player's left and right are the duelers
- The active / center player is the Judge
- Judge reveals one Rumble card
- First dueler to give 3 valid answers wins
- Winner gains one extra card from the TAKE ME pile
- Judge resolves disputed answers
- For letter-based challenges, answers cannot reuse the same meaningful word stem  
  - Example: `apple pie` followed by `apple juice` does not count as two separate answers

These do not need to be repeated on every Rumble card.

---

## Visual Role in the Game
RULE and ROLE should feel like two sides of the core game.

RUMBLE should feel different:
- Red
- Immediate
- Competitive
- Brief
- Visually disruptive without becoming cluttered


# Box Front Art Direction

## Overall Composition
Use three main visual fields:

- **RULE color** entering from one side
- **ROLE color** entering from the opposite side
- A generous **warm-white center area**

The green/purple fields can meet the center at slight angles rather than straight vertical edges.

This should echo the split visual language of the TAKE ME cards without simply copying a card front.

---

## Title

Keep the main title on one line:

**RULE ’N’ ROLE**

- **RULE** uses the Rule color and Rule typeface
- **ROLE** uses the Role color and Role typeface
- **’N’** can stay neutral / dark

Underneath:

**RUMBLE!**

- Large
- Red
- Strong and energetic
- Can use its own heavier / more disruptive display treatment

The title should visually communicate the game's three systems:

- RULE = structured
- ROLE = expressive
- RUMBLE = interruption / competition

---

## Tagline

Optional beneath the title:

**Pick your poison. Wing the rest.**

Keep this relatively small so it does not compete with the title.

Try the box both with and without it.

---

## General Direction

- Bold flat colors
- Strong typography
- Minimal illustration, if any
- Keep plenty of breathing room in the center
- Avoid filling every area with graphic elements
- Let the contrast between Rule / Role / Rumble provide most of the personality
- The box should clearly belong to the same visual system as the cards without looking like an oversized card
